Part A · Index minerals

Barrow's metamorphic zones

George Barrow mapped rocks in the Scottish Highlands that were metamorphosed by granitic intrusions. Walking away from the granite, he found mineral zones that tracked the intensity of metamorphism. His index minerals run from low to high pressure and temperature:

chlorite (lowest) biotite garnet staurolite kyanite sillimanite (highest)

The boundary between two zones is an isograd: a line of equal metamorphic grade, marked by the first appearance of an index mineral.

Part B · Polymorphs

Reading a pressure-temperature diagram

Metamorphism is driven mostly by rising pressure and temperature. A P-T (phase) diagram shows which index mineral is stable under which conditions. Andalusite, kyanite and sillimanite are polymorphs: same chemical formula (Al2SiO5), different crystal structure. Each is stable under its own range of P and T. Two can coexist only on the line between their fields. All three coexist only at the triple point, near 500 °C and 4 kbar, about 15 km deep.

Part C · Facies

Eskola's metamorphic facies

Finnish geologist Pentti Eskola showed that basalt metamorphoses into different facies (unique mineral assemblages) as pressure and temperature change.

Amphibolite

low P · high T

Black hornblende amphibole, sillimanite.

Greenschist

low P · low T

Green actinolite amphibole and chlorite.

Eclogite

high P · high T

Red garnet, green pyroxene.

Blueschist

high P · low T

Blue amphibole (glaucophane, riebeckite) and lawsonite.
