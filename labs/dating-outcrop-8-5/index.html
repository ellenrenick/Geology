Read the outcrop

This outcrop has four kinds of evidence. Compare the photo with the drawing before you answer any questions.

  1. Layers. Sandstone at the bottom, shale above it, then a basalt sill, on both sides of a fault zone. In the drawing, sedimentary rock that once covered the sill is shown on the right.
  2. Fossils. Fagopsis leaf fossils are present in the shale and sandstone. Leaf fossils like these are not index fossils for a single moment, but their time range is on Figure 8.13 in your lab manual.
  3. Zircon crystals inside the basalt can be dated with U-235 → Pb-207 decay (Figure 8.14).
  4. A fault. The layers on the right are offset from the same layers on the left.

The basalt is interpreted as a sill: magma that squeezed between existing layers and cooled in place. Keep that in mind when you decide what is older and what is younger.

8.5A · Question 2

Numerical age of the sill

Zircon crystals in the sill have 98.9% U-235 remaining and 1.10% Pb-207. The half-life of U-235 is 703.8 million years. The crystals probably formed when the sill cooled, so their age is also the age of the sill.

Each square is a share of the original U-235 atoms. Drag the slider to see how much of a half-life it takes for the daughter product to reach about 1%.

Step 1. What fraction of one half-life has elapsed? Figure 8.14C gives the value as 1/64. Enter it as a decimal.

Step 2. Multiply by the half-life to get the age of the sill, in millions of years.
